# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Primary users are parents and kids in the same household using a shared family task and rewards system across phones, tablets, and shared home screens. Parents need clear control over setup, assignments, safety settings, and accountability. Kids need an interface that feels fun, motivating, and easy to act on without confusion.

## Product Purpose
KidTasky helps families turn chores and routines into a consistent, engaging system that kids actually want to use while giving parents reliable oversight. Success means kids complete tasks more consistently, parents spend less time prompting, and family coordination (tasks, rewards, schedules, lists, meals) stays centralized in one trusted app UI.

## Brand Personality
Skylight-inspired, kid-focused, family-focused. The tone should feel bright, warm, and encouraging, with confident structure for parents and playful momentum for kids.

## Anti-references
- Generic B2B SaaS dashboard aesthetics
- Cold enterprise UI patterns that feel transactional or sterile
- Overly dense control panels that bury kid motivation under admin complexity

## Design Principles
- Motivation first, clarity always: make progress, rewards, and next actions immediately obvious to kids while keeping controls understandable for parents.
- Shared-family coherence: keep parent and kid experiences visually connected, but tuned for each role's decision load and goals.
- Safe by default: present permissions, lock states, and account boundaries clearly to reduce accidental misuse.
- Playful without noise: use delight and motion to reinforce actions and milestones, not to distract from task completion.
- Everyday resilience: design for quick check-ins throughout busy family routines, including mobile-first legibility and accessible interaction targets.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG AA is the baseline requirement for all core surfaces. Support high-contrast readability, consistent focus states, keyboard accessibility where applicable, reduced-motion accommodations, and color choices that remain understandable for common forms of color vision deficiency. Engagement for kids must not reduce accessibility or clarity.
